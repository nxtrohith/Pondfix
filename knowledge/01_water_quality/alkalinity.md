# Alkalinity

Alkalinity is the water's ability to neutralize acid and resist pH swings. In freshwater ponds it is mostly bicarbonate and carbonate, reported as mg/L as CaCO3.

## Why it matters
- It buffers the daily pH swing.
- It decides how pond water responds to liming.
- It supports biological productivity and, in some systems, the microbes that process ammonia.

## What can go wrong
Low alkalinity allows big pH swings. High alkalinity is not automatically good, especially alongside high pH, unsuitable salinity or other imbalances.

## Management
1. Measure alkalinity; do not guess it from pH.
2. Compare with the needs of the species and system.
3. Consider soil, water source, hardness and past liming.
4. Base any amendment on measurements and a professional recommendation.
5. Retest after the correction.

Agricultural limestone, hydrated lime and quicklime have different chemistry and hazards. They are not interchangeable and must not be given the same dose or purpose.
