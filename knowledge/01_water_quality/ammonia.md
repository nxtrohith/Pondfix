# Ammonia (TAN and un-ionized NH3)

Ammonia comes from fish waste and from microbes breaking down uneaten feed, faeces and other organic matter.

## Two forms
- **Ammonium (NH4+)**: much less toxic.
- **Un-ionized ammonia (NH3)**: crosses gill membranes easily and is the toxic form.
- **Total ammonia nitrogen (TAN)** is the sum of both. Test kits may report as nitrogen (NH3-N) or as ammonia (NH3). Check which one your kit reports; the numbers are not interchangeable.

## Why the same TAN can be safe in one pond and dangerous in another
The share of TAN that is toxic NH3 rises as **pH and temperature rise**. High pH in a warm afternoon pond is the worst case. Never judge a TAN number without pH and temperature.

## Provisional screening bands for un-ionized NH3 (not validated limits)
| Un-ionized NH3 | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| below 0.02 mg/L | lower concern |
| 0.02-0.05 mg/L | elevated: verify chemistry and look at the trend |
| 0.05 mg/L and above | high: investigate and correct promptly |
| 0.1 mg/L and above | very high: urgent assessment by an operator or professional |

These are conservative screening bands for a prototype, not universal toxicity thresholds. Tolerance depends on species, exposure time and water conditions.

## Causes
Overfeeding and uneaten feed, high stocking density, accumulated sludge, weak biological filtration in recirculating systems, sudden changes to the pond's ecology.

## Effects
Gill damage, breathing distress, restlessness, poor feeding and growth, weaker disease resistance, and deaths at high exposure. These signs also fit low oxygen and infection.

## Response
1. Confirm the TAN result and the kit's reporting basis.
2. Check pH, temperature, DO and recent feeding.
3. Cut or pause feeding if waste load is the likely cause.
4. Remove accumulated organic waste where practical and safe, and keep aeration on.
5. Replace water only after checking the incoming water's chemistry, temperature and disease risk.
6. Do not raise pH with lime to "fix" ammonia: it can increase the toxic NH3 share. Aeration helps oxygen and treatment but does not guarantee ammonia removal.
7. Get expert guidance for large or persistent elevations.
