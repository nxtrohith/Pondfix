# Water temperature

Temperature controls metabolism, appetite, growth, immune function, oxygen availability and the toxicity of some compounds.

## Why it matters
- Warmer water holds less dissolved oxygen, while many fish use oxygen faster as it warms (within their comfortable range).
- Cold water can suppress feeding and growth in warm-water species.
- Rapid change causes stress, even when both values are inside the target range.
- Temperature changes how much ammonia is in the toxic un-ionized form.

## Causes of dangerous temperatures
Heat waves, shallow water, sudden cold weather, large inflows of water at a different temperature, low water volume, no shade where that applies, and seasonal or monsoon swings.

## Response
1. Verify the value and where the sensor sits. An air-temperature sensor above the pond is not a water-temperature reading.
2. Compare with the species and life-stage range. A high temperature alone does not show that fish are diseased.
3. Read DO together with temperature, never temperature alone.
4. Avoid abrupt water replacement that could cause thermal shock.
5. Escalate extreme or persistent deviations to a qualified aquaculture professional.

## For monitoring
Track the rate of change as well as the value. A fast rise or fall can deserve attention before any absolute limit is crossed.
