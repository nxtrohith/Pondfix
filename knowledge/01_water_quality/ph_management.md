# pH

pH shows how acidic or alkaline the water is. 7 is neutral, below 7 is acidic, above 7 is alkaline. The scale is logarithmic: one unit is a tenfold change. Many freshwater fish are kept in roughly pH 6.5-8.5 as a general starting range; the right range depends on species and system.

## Why pond pH changes during the day
Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide in daylight and raises pH; respiration releases it at night and lowers pH. An afternoon reading can therefore differ a lot from a pre-dawn reading, so compare readings taken at the same time of day.

## Causes of abnormal pH
- Dense algae (high afternoon pH) or heavy respiration and carbon dioxide (low pH).
- Rain or inflow water, soil type, low alkalinity, liming history.
- Chemical discharges or inputs.

## Effects
- Extreme pH can damage gills and disturb normal body function.
- High pH raises the share of ammonia that is the toxic un-ionized form.
- Rapid change is stressful even if the final value is inside the target range.
- An abnormal pH does not by itself diagnose an infectious disease.

## Response
1. Confirm the reading with a calibrated meter and check the sensor for fouling.
2. Compare with earlier readings, ideally morning versus afternoon.
3. Check alkalinity, ammonia, recent rain, algae and any chemicals added.
4. Do not add lime, acid or other chemicals because of one unusual reading.
5. Get expert advice before correcting a large or fast change.
