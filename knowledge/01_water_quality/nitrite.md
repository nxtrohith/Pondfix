# Nitrite (NO2-)

Nitrite is an intermediate in the biological conversion of ammonia to nitrate. It builds up when that conversion is overloaded, immature or disrupted.

## Why it rises
- Biological filtration or the pond's microbial community is immature or impaired.
- Feed and waste load exceed treatment capacity.
- Water chemistry changes disturb the microbes.
- Stocking and feeding were increased faster than the system could adapt.

## Effect on fish
Nitrite interferes with oxygen transport in the blood by forming methemoglobin ("brown blood disease"). Fish can look oxygen-starved even when DO is fine.

## Warning signs
Lethargy, poor feeding, abnormal swimming and breathing distress. Brown blood in severe cases is not a test a farmer should rely on.

## Response
1. Confirm with a proper nitrite test.
2. Check ammonia, DO, pH and the filtration or treatment system.
3. Reduce waste loading and review feeding. Keep aeration on.
4. Get expert advice before adding salt or other chemicals: a safe treatment depends on species, salinity, water chemistry and system.

## Units
Nitrite reported as NO2- and as nitrite-nitrogen (NO2-N) are different numbers. Always record which one a result uses. PondFix does not currently measure nitrite; show it as "Not measured" rather than assuming it is safe.
