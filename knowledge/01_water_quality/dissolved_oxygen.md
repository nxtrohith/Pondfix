# Dissolved oxygen (DO)

Dissolved oxygen is the oxygen in the water that fish and shrimp breathe, usually measured in mg/L. It is the most time-critical water-quality parameter: oxygen shortage can cause distress and deaths within hours.

## Why DO changes
- Oxygen enters through the air-water surface, photosynthesis by algae and plants in daylight, and aerators.
- Oxygen is used by fish, shrimp, microbes, decomposing feed and waste, and pond-bottom sediment.
- At night photosynthesis stops while respiration continues, so DO is usually lowest just before sunrise.
- Dense stocking, excess feed, organic sludge, cloudy weather and an algal die-off raise the risk.
- Warm water holds less oxygen while animals need more of it as temperature rises.

## Warning signs
- Fish gather at the surface or near water inlets and gasp.
- Fast gill movement, swimming that looks abnormal, loss of balance, weakness.
- Feeding drops. Several animals are affected at once.

These signs are not specific. Ammonia, nitrite, gill disease or a bloom collapse can look the same, so verify with a calibrated meter.

## Farmer response
1. If fish are visibly distressed, start or increase aeration now if it is available and safe. Do not wait to finish a diagnosis.
2. Measure DO with a calibrated instrument at the depth where the animals are, especially before dawn.
3. Check that the aerator is running and that power or fuel has not failed.
4. Stop or cut feeding during an acute oxygen problem until things settle.
5. Keep monitoring. Contact a fisheries expert if distress continues or fish die.

## Limits and thresholds
There is no single DO limit for every species, life stage, system and exposure time. Provisional working targets for each species are in the species profiles; they are reference values, not validated alert limits. Use the pond's configured limits, the trend and the time of day together.
