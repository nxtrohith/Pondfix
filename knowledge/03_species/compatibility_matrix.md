# Polyculture and species compatibility

Polyculture means raising compatible species in one pond to use food and habitat efficiently without harmful competition or predation.

## Indian major carps
Rohu, catla and mrigal are traditionally combined because they mostly use different feeding niches: catla high in the water column (zooplankton), rohu mid-column, mrigal near the bottom. Real compatibility and ratios depend on fish size, pond productivity, goals and management. This library gives no stocking ratios.

## Before recommending a combination, check
- Can one species eat another? (Walking catfish is predatory towards small fish.)
- Do they compete for the same food?
- Do their temperature, oxygen and water-chemistry needs differ?
- Growth rates and harvest sizes.
- Breeding behaviour and the risk of uncontrolled spawning.
- Disease spread and biosecurity.
- Local rules, and whether an introduced species is allowed.

A species is not "universally compatible" just because it worked with another in one system. Ask the farmer for the species, sizes and system before giving a firm answer, and suggest a local fisheries officer for introductions of new species.
