# Rohu (Labeo rohita)

An Indian major carp, widely farmed in freshwater ponds, often together with catla and mrigal (composite carp culture). A good species for building a baseline of normal swimming so deviations can be spotted.

## Provisional working ranges (reference only, not validated alert limits)
| Parameter | Working range | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temperature | about 24-31 C | preferred growth range, not a mortality limit |
| pH | about 6.5-8.5 | general freshwater starting range |
| Dissolved oxygen | aim for 5 mg/L or more | proposed early warning below 4, high priority below 3 mg/L |
| Un-ionized NH3 | keep very low | see the ammonia guide |

A published welfare study identified about 3 mg/L DO as a welfare threshold for rohu under its study conditions. Surviving below a threshold does not mean conditions are acceptable. Values change with life stage, density, feeding intensity and system.

## Health problems to know
- **Oxygen deficiency**: fish at the surface, rapid gill movement, weak swimming, poor feeding. Prevent with aeration, sensible density, removing organic waste and checking DO before dawn.
- **Argulosis (fish lice)**: rubbing, restlessness, skin damage. Rohu is reported to be particularly susceptible. See `argulosis`.
- **Bacterial ulcers and haemorrhages** (Aeromonas and other opportunists): see `aeromoniasis`.
- **Myxoboliasis**: parasitic; see `myxoboliasis`.

## Behaviour and risk rules (for the assistant)
- More surface swimming plus low DO points to oxygen deficiency.
- Lasting reduction in movement with abnormal temperature or pH points to general stress.
- Motionless fish with abnormal conditions need urgent inspection; do not assume they are dead.
- Skin lesions and parasites need images or a person to inspect them. They cannot be inferred from water readings.
