# Farmer FAQ

Each answer gives likely explanations, an immediate precaution, what information would help, and when to call an expert.

**Why are my fish coming to the surface?** Low dissolved oxygen is the first thing to rule out, especially near dawn or in hot, cloudy weather. Start aeration if you can, measure DO, stop feeding for now. Walking catfish can surface normally to breathe air. Ammonia, nitrite and disease can look similar. Call an expert if it continues or fish die.

**Why are my fish not eating?** Low oxygen, heat or cold, poor water quality, disease, spoiled feed or stress. Check DO, temperature and pH, and recent changes. Do not keep adding feed.

**What if the aerator's power fails?** Switch to backup, stop feeding, watch for gasping, measure DO once safe, and call an expert if fish show distress.

**Why is the water suddenly green?** Usually an algal bloom. Measure DO (including before dawn), note weather and feeding, and do not apply sudden chemical treatments, since a mass die-off can strip the oxygen.

**Is it safe to feed during heavy rain?** Cut feed when conditions are changing and fish activity falls; check DO, pH and temperature, then resume gradually.

**Why is the water level falling?** Leakage, an open outlet, evaporation or low supply. Check the reading is steady, then inspect embankments and outlets.

**Can I add lime?** Only after measuring pH, alkalinity and ammonia. Lime can raise the toxic ammonia share when pH is already high. Get a professional rate.

**Can I introduce a new species?** Ask a local fisheries officer. Check predation, competition, disease risk and local rules.

**What does a critical alert mean?** A reading crossed the configured critical limit. Read the alert details and verify the reading. If fish are gasping or dying, treat it as an emergency.

**Why did my app stop updating?** See the stale-data guide: phone, device, Wi-Fi and backend can each fail separately.

**How do I know a reading is current?** Check its timestamp and freshness label.

**When should I contact a fisheries officer?** Gasping or dying fish, spreading lesions, many fish affected quickly, a suspected spill, or any plan to use medicine or chemicals.
