# Disease: Aeromoniasis (bacterial ulcer and haemorrhagic disease)

## Classification
- Category: bacterial disease and opportunistic infection.
- Causative agent: Aeromonas species and other opportunistic bacteria. These occur naturally in water; disease is more likely when fish are stressed.
- Evidence status: provisional, pending expert review.

## Clinical signs (all non-specific)
Skin ulcers, red spots and haemorrhages, fin damage, swollen belly, lethargy, abnormal swimming, reduced activity. Parasites, injuries and other infections look similar.

## Likely causes and risk factors
Poor water quality, low oxygen, crowding, handling injuries, parasite damage. Poor water quality contributes to stress but does not prove a specific bacterial infection.

## Water-quality associations
Check DO, temperature, ammonia and recent changes.

## Diagnostic steps
Record how many fish are affected and how fast it is spreading. Photograph lesions. Confirmation needs examination and laboratory testing.

## Immediate precautions
Remove dead fish promptly with safe biosecurity. Avoid moving stock, nets and equipment between ponds. Reduce handling and other stress.

## Treatment and prevention
Keep water quality good, avoid overcrowding and handling injuries, and investigate unusual deaths early. Do not recommend antibiotics without a diagnosis and authorised treatment advice.

## Escalation
Contact a veterinarian or aquatic animal health lab when lesions appear, several fish are affected, or deaths rise.
