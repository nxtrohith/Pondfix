# Stocking density

Stocking density is how many animals, or how much biomass, a pond holds: fish per square metre, fish per hectare, or kg per cubic metre depending on the system. There is no universal density. Higher density raises demand for oxygen, feed management and waste processing, so a pond that is fine with reliable aeration may not be without it.

## It depends on
Species and life stage, target harvest size, pond depth, area and water exchange, aeration capacity and reliability, feed quality and daily feed, natural productivity, and your monitoring and emergency capacity.

## Before stocking
1. Confirm the species and culture system.
2. Assess the pond and the water source.
3. Estimate target harvest biomass.
4. Check aeration and a backup for power failure.
5. Plan water-quality monitoring and biosecurity.
6. Take stocking figures from a regional, species-specific production guide. This library does not give numbers.

Count and biomass are different: the same number of fish has very different oxygen demand at 20 g and at 800 g.
